# P03 report: ARPAbet → espeak IPA mapping

Generated by `python -m phoneset.roundtrip_test`; edit `REPORT.template.md`, not this
file. Tools: g2p_en 2.1.0, phonemizer 3.4.0, espeak-ng 1.52.0. Model vocab: `facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft`.

## Result

- `check_vocab.py`: every token the table can emit is in the model `vocab.json` (100%).
- Round trip on the 200 most frequent NGSL lemmas (`ngsl_head200.txt`), g2p_en + table
  vs espeak-ng en-us, one word at a time:
  - **strict agreement: 176/200 (88.0%)** (identical token lists after removing stress marks)
  - **agreement after the fold rules: 186/200 (93.0%)** (see Method)
  - **14 words still differ**, all listed and classified below. No mapping bug
    is left in them.
- Words outside CMUdict (where g2p_en falls back to its neural model): 0 of 200.
  So this test says nothing about g2p_en's guesses for unseen words.
- Spike cross-check: 0 of 8 hand-written spike lexicon entries disagree
  with the table or with espeak-ng (see below).

## Method

1. `g2p_en` gives ARPAbet with stress digits. `mapping.to_espeak` converts it with
   `arpabet_to_espeak.yaml`.
2. `phonemizer` (espeak-ng, `en-us`, phone separator on) gives espeak's own token
   segmentation, so multi-character units such as `ɑːɹ` and `əl` arrive as single
   tokens, exactly as in the model's training labels. Stress marks are removed.
3. **Strict**: the two token lists are equal.
4. **Fold rules**, applied to both sides, each of which can only make two lists more
   equal (`compare.py`):
   - *syllabic/glottal*: `əl` → `ə l`, `n̩` → `ə n`, `ʔ` → `t`
   - *reduced vowel*: `ə ɐ ᵻ ɪ` are one class (the vowel of "family" is ɪ or ə by
     speaker; ɐ and ᵻ are espeak's own reduction symbols)
   - *flap*: `t d ɾ` are one class between two vowels ("water", "city")

   The reduced-vowel and flap folds are blunt: they do not check stress, so a stressed
   ɪ against ə would also be hidden. It did not matter here; it may in P25.
5. Words that differ after folding are labelled by hand in `mismatch_labels.yaml`
   as `accent variant`, `g2p error` or `mapping bug`. The class is a judgement from
   the two token lists, not a measurement. `roundtrip_test.py` exits non-zero on an
   unlabelled word or a `mapping bug`.

## Mapping bugs found and fixed

The first version of the table mapped each phone independently. Comparing with
espeak-ng on the head-200 list and on the full NGSL (2809 lemmas, not committed:
CC BY-SA, download from newgeneralservicelist.com) exposed context rules that a
per-phone table cannot express. Agreement measured on 2026-09-29:

| Table | Head 200 strict | Head 200 folded | Full NGSL strict | Full NGSL folded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-phone only | 154 (77.0%) | 161 (80.5%) | 1476 (52.5%) | 1949 (69.4%) |
| with the rules below | 176 (88.0%) | 186 (93.0%) | 1837 (65.4%) | 2460 (87.6%) |

- **Unstressed IY**: "happy" is `h æ p i`, not `iː`; before a vowel it stays `iː`
  ("create"); before a consonant espeak reduces it to `ɪ` ("anything"). By far the
  largest single source of error (200 words in the full list).
- **Vowel + R**: espeak emits `ɑːɹ ɔːɹ ɛɹ ɪɹ ʊɹ` as one token when the R ends the word
  or precedes a consonant ("car", "near"), and keeps vowel and R apart before a vowel
  ("very" `v ɛ ɹ i`). `UH` is the exception: "during" is `d ʊɹ ɹ ɪ ŋ`.
- **CMUdict IY + R** is the NEAR set ("here", "hear", "ear") and maps to `ɪɹ`.
- **AY + ER0 / R** → `aɪɚ` ("fire", "retire"); **AY + AH0** → `aɪə` ("science");
  **IY + AH0** → `iə` ("area", "idea"). All three tokens are in the vocab.
- **ER before a vowel** is followed by an extra `ɹ` ("around" `ɚ ɹ aʊ n d`).

## Choices that depend on the accent

All are marked `ACCENT` in the YAML. Summary: US English, rhotic; `AA` is `ɑː` (no
`ɒ`); `AO` is `ɔː`; `OW` is `oʊ`; `T` and `D` are never mapped to the flap `ɾ` or the
glottal stop `ʔ`, although espeak-ng and most US speakers produce both in
"water", "city", "button". The reference is therefore the careful citation form.

## Consequences for P22 and P25

These follow from the vocab and the label conventions seen above. None has been
tested against model output yet; P22 and P25 should test them first.

- **Weak vowels.** The vocab holds espeak's own symbols (`ᵻ`, `ɐ`, `əl`), so the
  training labels were espeak output, and espeak spells the same unstressed vowel
  `ə ɐ ᵻ ɪ` depending on the word (`ɛ` after "ex-": "expect" is `ɛ k s p ˈɛ k t`,
  about 50 words in the full list). A single reference token for an unstressed vowel
  is likely to give false errors on correct speech. Score the best token of the class,
  or skip unstressed vowels.
- **Flap.** US speakers and espeak-ng use `ɾ` in "water", and the table asks for `t`.
  Score /t/ and /d/ between vowels as the best of `t d ɾ`.
- **Syllabic l and n.** espeak labels "little" with the single token `əl`; the table
  gives `ə l`. If the model emits `əl`, scoring `l` alone after schwa will fail on
  native speech.
- Lexical vowel differences between CMUdict and espeak-ng (`ɔː` / `ɔ` / `ɑː`, `æ` / `ɛ`
  before r) are real but small: about 60 of the 2809 lemmas.
- 349 of 2809 NGSL lemmas (12.4%) still differ after folding on the full list. They
  were not classified individually; the head-200 sample is weighted toward function words
  and short common words.
